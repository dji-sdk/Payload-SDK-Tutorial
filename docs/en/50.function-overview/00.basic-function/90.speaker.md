## Overview

PSDK provides standard speaker function interfaces. Developers can implement voice speaking, TTS (text-to-speech) speaking, volume and playback mode control by registering callback functions, and users can directly operate the speaker from the DJI Pilot side.

:::tip Note:
Before using the speaker widget, you need to enable the <a href="./custom-widget.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">custom widget</a> first.
:::

## Basic Concepts

### Speaker Widget

DJI Pilot 2 supports displaying the standard speaker widget. Users can control whether to display the icon by configuring the speaker json field of <a href="./custom-widget.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">custom widget</a>.

The speaker widget supports the following operations:

**Voice Speaking**

Users can record the content that needs to be spoken through the "press to speak" button. During the recording, the Pilot has timekeeping. When the time is close to the maximum recording time, the remote controller will vibrate and have other interactive notifications. When the recording is finished, after the user presses the "Stop" button, the "local audition" or "speak" button can be pressed to play. In addition, when the recording result is not satisfying, it can be recorded again, or the recording audio file can be saved and managed through the audio list (audition, file information display, deletion, rename, upload and play are supported).

|Maximum 3 min|16kHz, mono, 16bit|opus@16kbps, single frame data 160 bytes|

**Hotkey Speaking**

### TTS

TTS is the abbreviation of Text To Speech, which is "from text to speech", which is a part of human-machine dialogue, a text-to-speech technology that enables machines to speak. At present, the development of TTS technology is relatively mature, mainly including two types, one is audio synthesis through dedicated TTS hardware chips, and the other can be converted and synthesized through TTS online or offline software libraries, such as [Xunfei Offline Speech Synthesis SDK](https://www.xfyun.cn/doc/tts/offline_tts/Linux-SDK.html).

### PCM Data

The raw data format of audio is Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) data. It is a digital signal produced by sampling, quantizing and encoding a continuously changing analog signal. Describing a piece of PCM data generally requires the following concepts: quantization format, sampling rate (sampleRate), and number of channels (channel).

### Opus Audio Codec

Opus is a completely open, royalty-free, versatile audio codec. It is suitable for interactive voice and music transmission over the Internet, but also for storage and streaming applications.

The predecessor of Opus is the celt encoder. In today's competition for lossy audio formats, the AAC format with many different encoders has defeated the equally promising formats such as Musepack and Vorbis, but after the birth of the Opus format, the situation seems to be different. Through many comparative tests, Opus completely outperformed HE AAC, which used to have obvious advantages at low bit rates. At medium bit rates, it was already comparable to the AAC format with a bit rate that was about 30% higher than that of the enemy, while at high bit rates it was closer to the original audio.

Opus can handle a variety of audio applications, including voice over IP, video conferencing, in-game chat, and even remote live music performances. It can scale from low bit rate narrowband speech to very high quality stereo music. Supported features include:

* Bit rate from 6kb/s to 510 kb/s
* Sampling rates from 8kHz (narrowband) to 48kHz (full band)
* Frame size from 2.5ms to 60ms
* Support Constant Bit Rate (CBR) and Variable Bit Rate (VBR)
* Audio bandwidth from narrow to full band
* Support voice and music
* Support mono and stereo
* Support up to 255 channels (multi-stream frame)
* Dynamically adjustable bit rate, audio bandwidth and frame size
* Good robustness and concealment
* Floating point and fixed point implementation

### Sampling Rate

The audio sampling rate refers to the number of times the recording device samples the sound signal in one second. The higher the sampling frequency, the more realistic and natural the sound will be. On today's mainstream capture cards, the sampling frequency is generally divided into five levels: 11025Hz, 22050Hz, 24000Hz, 44100Hz, and 48000Hz

The frequency corresponds to the time axis, and the amplitude corresponds to the level axis. The wave is infinitely smooth, and the string can be regarded as composed of countless points. Since the storage space is relatively limited, the points of the string must be sampled in the process of digital encoding. The sampling process is to extract the frequency value of a certain point. Obviously, the more points are extracted in one second, the more abundant frequency information can be obtained. **In order to restore the waveform, there must be 2 points of sampling in one vibration. The highest frequency that the human ear can perceive is 20kHz, so to meet the hearing requirements of the human ear, at least 40k samples per second are required, expressed in 40kHz, and this 40kHz is the sampling rate. Our common CD has a sampling rate of 44.1kHz.**

For the speaker payload mounted on the drone, audio encoding needs to be performed from the ground-side remote controller and uploaded to the aircraft side. In order to maximize the transmission efficiency, a trade-off between sound quality and sampling rate is required.

### Channel

Channels refer to independent audio signals that are collected or played back at different spatial positions during recording or playback, so the number of channels is the number of sound sources during sound recording or the number of corresponding speakers during playback.

For the speaker payload mounted on the drone, due to the limitation of the drone's load and power, a single-speaker solution, that is, a single channel, is generally selected.

### Speaker Audio Parameters

Due to the load, power and uplink limitations of the drone, the speaker function limits the relevant parameters of the audio in order to meet the needs of various usage scenarios.

* Number of channels: 1
* Sampling rate: 16 kHz
* Bit width: 16 bit

### State Reporting

The SDK periodically calls the `GetSpeakerState` callback to get the current speaker state (playback state, work mode, play mode, volume) and reports it to the DJI Pilot side for display.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode GetSpeakerState(T_DjiWidgetSpeakerState *speakerState)
{
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();

    osalHandler->MutexLock(s_speakerMutex);
    *speakerState = s_speakerState;
    osalHandler->MutexUnlock(s_speakerMutex);

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

### Mode and Volume Setting

After registering callbacks, the SDK will automatically call the following callback functions at runtime to set the speaker's work mode, play mode and volume. Developers need to implement these callbacks and protect the read/write operations on state variables using mutex locks.

#### Set Work Mode

When the user switches between TTS / Voice mode on the DJI Pilot side, the SDK calls the `SetWorkMode` callback to notify the payload device.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode SetWorkMode(E_DjiWidgetSpeakerWorkMode workMode)
{
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();

    osalHandler->MutexLock(s_speakerMutex);
    USER_LOG_INFO("Set widget speaker work mode: %d", workMode);
    s_speakerState.workMode = workMode;
    osalHandler->MutexUnlock(s_speakerMutex);

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

#### Set Play Mode

When the user switches between Single / Loop playback on the DJI Pilot side, the SDK calls the `SetPlayMode` callback to notify the payload device.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode SetPlayMode(E_DjiWidgetSpeakerPlayMode playMode)
{
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();

    osalHandler->MutexLock(s_speakerMutex);
    USER_LOG_INFO("Set widget speaker play mode: %d", playMode);
    s_speakerState.playMode = playMode;
    osalHandler->MutexUnlock(s_speakerMutex);

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

#### Set Volume

For the control of speaker audio volume, the implementation needs to be adapted to the specific speaker payload hardware. The following example sets the sound card volume through the `pactl set-sink-volume` command to achieve audio volume control.

```c
    T_DjiReturnCode returnCode;
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();
    char cmdStr[128];
    int32_t ret = 0;
    float realVolume;

    realVolume = 1.5f * (float) volume;
    USER_LOG_INFO("Set widget speaker volume: %d", volume);
    s_speakerState.volume = volume;

    memset(cmdStr, 0, sizeof(cmdStr));
    snprintf(cmdStr, sizeof(cmdStr), "pactl set-sink-volume %s %d%%", WIDGET_SPEAKER_USB_AUDIO_DEVICE_NAME,
             (int32_t) realVolume);

    returnCode = DjiUserUtil_RunSystemCmd(cmdStr);
    if (returnCode < 0) {
        USER_LOG_ERROR("Set widget speaker volume error: %d", ret);
    }
```

### Data Reception

Voice speaking and TTS speaking share the same data reception mechanism. The APP transmits data to the payload device through SDK callbacks. The transmission process is divided into three stages:

<div style="text-align: center"><p><span>
      <img src="https://terra-1-g.djicdn.com/71a7d383e71a4fb8887a310eb746b47f/psdk/function-overview/speaker/speaker_transfer_1_en.svg" width="30%" alt="Speaker Data Reception Common Flow"/></span></p>
</div>

- **START**: The APP sends a start command, and the speaker creates a file to prepare for reception.
- **TRANSMIT** (repeatable): The APP sends the data offset, length and content, and the speaker writes to the file at the specified offset.
- **FINISH**: The APP sends the MD5 value, and the speaker verifies data integrity. After voice data passes verification, Opus decoding is performed; TTS data proceeds directly to audio synthesis.

#### Receive Voice Audio Data

After the user records audio or imports an audio file on the DJI Pilot side, the SDK transmits the Opus-encoded audio data to the payload device through the `ReceiveVoiceData` callback.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode ReceiveAudioData(E_DjiWidgetTransmitDataEvent event,
                                        uint32_t offset, uint8_t *buf, uint16_t size)
{
    uint16_t writeLen;
    T_DjiReturnCode returnCode;

    if (event == DJI_WIDGET_TRANSMIT_DATA_EVENT_START) {
        USER_LOG_INFO("Create voice file.");
        audioFile = fopen(WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_FILE_NAME, "wb");
        if (audioFile == NULL) {
            USER_LOG_ERROR("Create tts file error.");
        }
    } else if (event == DJI_WIDGET_TRANSMIT_DATA_EVENT_TRANSMIT) {
        USER_LOG_INFO("Transmit voice file, offset: %d, size: %d", offset, size);
        if (audioFile != NULL) {
            fseek(audioFile, offset, SEEK_SET);
            writeLen = fwrite(buf, 1, size, audioFile);
            if (writeLen != size) {
                USER_LOG_ERROR("Write tts file error %d", writeLen);
            }
        }
    } else if (event == DJI_WIDGET_TRANSMIT_DATA_EVENT_FINISH) {
        USER_LOG_INFO("Close voice file.");
        if (audioFile != NULL) {
            fclose(audioFile);
        }

        returnCode = DjiTest_CheckFileMd5Sum(WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_FILE_NAME, buf, size);
        if (returnCode != DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS) {
            USER_LOG_ERROR("File md5 sum check failed");
        }

        DjiTest_DecodeAudioData();
    }

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

#### Receive TTS Text Data

After the user enters or imports text on the DJI Pilot side, the SDK transmits the text data to the payload device through the `ReceiveTtsData` callback. The transmission process is the same as voice speaking, also divided into START, TRANSMIT, and FINISH stages, except that the transmitted content is text data rather than Opus audio.

#### Start Playback

When the user clicks play on the DJI Pilot side, the SDK calls the `StartPlay` callback. The implementation needs to first stop the currently playing audio process, then set the speaker state to `PLAYING`. The background playback task will start playing audio upon detecting this state.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode StartPlay(void)
{
    uint32_t pid;
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();

    pid = DjiTest_GetVoicePlayProcessId();
    if (pid != 0) {
        DjiTest_KillVoicePlayProcess(pid);
    }

    osalHandler->TaskSleepMs(5);
    USER_LOG_INFO("Start widget speaker play");
    SetSpeakerState(DJI_WIDGET_SPEAKER_STATE_PLAYING);

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

#### Stop Playback

When the user clicks stop on the DJI Pilot side, the SDK calls the `StopPlay` callback. The implementation needs to set the state to `IDLE` and terminate the current playback process.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode StopPlay(void)
{
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();
    uint32_t pid;

    osalHandler->MutexLock(s_speakerMutex);
    USER_LOG_INFO("Stop widget speaker play");
    s_speakerState.state = DJI_WIDGET_SPEAKER_STATE_IDEL;

    pid = DjiTest_GetVoicePlayProcessId();
    if (pid != 0) {
        DjiTest_KillVoicePlayProcess(pid);
    }

    osalHandler->MutexUnlock(s_speakerMutex);

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

## Sample Tutorial

The following examples demonstrate the complete initialization flow of the speaker function, as well as the end-to-end implementation of two typical usage scenarios: voice speaking and TTS speaking.

### Initialize Speaker Service

Before using the speaker function, you need to first configure the widget json to enable the speaker icon, then register the callback function set and initialize the state.

**Configure Widget JSON**

Control the speaker icon display on the DJI Pilot side through the json field in the speaker option:

```json
  "main_interface": {
    "floating_window": {
      "is_enable": true
    },
    "speaker": {
      "is_enable_tts": true,
      "is_enable_voice": true
    },
```

**Register Callbacks and Initialize State**

Bind each callback function to the `T_DjiWidgetSpeakerHandler` structure, then call `DjiWidget_RegSpeakerHandler` to register. After registration, initialize the speaker state and default volume, and start the background playback task.

```c
T_DjiReturnCode DjiTest_WidgetSpeakerStartService(void)
{
    T_DjiReturnCode returnCode;
    T_DjiOsalHandler *osalHandler = DjiPlatform_GetOsalHandler();

    // Bind callback functions
    s_speakerHandler.GetSpeakerState = GetSpeakerState;
    s_speakerHandler.SetWorkMode = SetWorkMode;
    s_speakerHandler.StartPlay = StartPlay;
    s_speakerHandler.StopPlay = StopPlay;
    s_speakerHandler.SetPlayMode = SetPlayMode;
    s_speakerHandler.SetVolume = SetVolume;
    s_speakerHandler.ReceiveTtsData = ReceiveTtsData;
    s_speakerHandler.ReceiveVoiceData = ReceiveAudioData;

    // Create mutex
    returnCode = osalHandler->MutexCreate(&s_speakerMutex);
    if (returnCode != DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS) {
        USER_LOG_ERROR("Create speaker mutex error: 0x%08llX", returnCode);
        return returnCode;
    }

    // Register speaker handler
    returnCode = DjiWidget_RegSpeakerHandler(&s_speakerHandler);
    if (returnCode != DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS) {
        USER_LOG_ERROR("Register speaker handler error: 0x%08llX", returnCode);
        return returnCode;
    }

    // Initialize state
    osalHandler->MutexLock(s_speakerMutex);
    s_speakerState.state = DJI_WIDGET_SPEAKER_STATE_IDEL;
    s_speakerState.workMode = DJI_WIDGET_SPEAKER_WORK_MODE_VOICE;
    s_speakerState.playMode = DJI_WIDGET_SPEAKER_PLAY_MODE_SINGLE_PLAY;
    osalHandler->MutexUnlock(s_speakerMutex);

    // Set default volume
    SetVolume(WIDGET_SPEAKER_DEFAULT_VOLUME);

    // Start background playback task
    osalHandler->TaskCreate("user_widget_speaker_task", DjiTest_WidgetSpeakerTask,
                            WIDGET_SPEAKER_TASK_STACK_SIZE, NULL, &s_widgetSpeakerTestThread);

    return DJI_ERROR_SYSTEM_MODULE_CODE_SUCCESS;
}
```

### Voice Speaking

The complete flow for voice speaking is: User records audio or imports an audio file on the Pilot side -> SDK transmits Opus audio data through the `ReceiveVoiceData` callback -> Payload side decodes to PCM -> Play audio.

**Receive Opus Audio Data**

The SDK transmits the Opus-encoded audio data to the payload device through the `ReceiveVoiceData` callback. The reception process is divided into START -> TRANSMIT -> FINISH stages (see the [Data Reception](#data-reception) section for details). After MD5 verification in the FINISH stage, the decode function is called.

**Opus Decoding**

Decode the Opus-encoded data into PCM format. The following is example code for the Linux platform that creates an Opus decoder, reads Opus data frame by frame, and stores the decoded data as a PCM file.

> Note: For detailed usage instructions of the Opus decoder, please refer to [Opus API Documentation](https://www.opus-codec.org/docs/opus_api-1.3.1/group__opus__decoder.html).

```c
    FILE *fin;
    FILE *fout;
    OpusDecoder *decoder;
    opus_int16 out[WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_MAX_FRAME_SIZE * WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_CHANNELS];
    uint8_t cbits[WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_MAX_PACKET_SIZE];
    int32_t nbBytes;
    int32_t err;

    /*! Attention: you can use "ffmpeg -i xxx.mp3 -ar 16000 -ac 1 out.wav" and use opus-tools to generate opus file for test */
    fin = fopen(WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_FILE_NAME, "r");
    if (fin == NULL) {
        fprintf(stderr, "failed to open input file: %s\n", strerror(errno));
        return EXIT_FAILURE;
    }

    /* Create a new decoder state. */
    decoder = opus_decoder_create(WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_SAMPLE_RATE, WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_CHANNELS, &err);
    if (err < 0) {
        fprintf(stderr, "failed to create decoder: %s\n", opus_strerror(err));
        return EXIT_FAILURE;
    }

    fout = fopen(WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_PCM_FILE_NAME, "w");
    if (fout == NULL) {
        fprintf(stderr, "failed to open output file: %s\n", strerror(errno));
        return EXIT_FAILURE;
    }

    USER_LOG_INFO("Decode Start...");

    while (1) {
        int i;
        unsigned char pcm_bytes[WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_MAX_FRAME_SIZE * WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_CHANNELS * 2];
        int frame_size;

        /* Read a 16 bits/sample audio frame. */
        nbBytes = fread(cbits, 1, WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_DECODE_FRAME_SIZE, fin);
        if (feof(fin))
            break;

        frame_size = opus_decode(decoder, cbits, nbBytes, out, WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_MAX_FRAME_SIZE, 0);
        if (frame_size < 0) {
            fprintf(stderr, "decoder failed: %s\n", opus_strerror(frame_size));
            return EXIT_FAILURE;
        }

        /* Convert to little-endian ordering. */
        for (i = 0; i < WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_CHANNELS * frame_size; i++) {
            pcm_bytes[2 * i] = out[i] & 0xFF;
            pcm_bytes[2 * i + 1] = (out[i] >> 8) & 0xFF;
        }
        /* Write the decoded audio to file. */
        fwrite(pcm_bytes, sizeof(short), frame_size * WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_OPUS_CHANNELS, fout);
    }

    /*Destroy the encoder state*/
    opus_decoder_destroy(decoder);
    fclose(fin);
    fclose(fout);
```

**Play Audio**

After decoding is complete, play the PCM audio data. The implementation needs to be adapted to the specific speaker payload hardware. There are two commonly used methods:

- Digital to analog I2S output: For example, on RTOS platforms, a dedicated digital PA chip is generally used to complete audio playback through I2S.
- System audio output: For Linux platforms that support audio-related drivers by default, you only need to operate the sound card through upper-layer software for audio playback, such as ffplay.

```c
static T_DjiReturnCode DjiTest_PlayAudioData(void)
{
    char cmdStr[128];

    memset(cmdStr, 0, sizeof(cmdStr));

    snprintf(cmdStr, sizeof(cmdStr), "ffplay -nodisp -autoexit -ar 16000 -ac 1 -f s16le -i %s 2>/dev/null",
             WIDGET_SPEAKER_AUDIO_PCM_FILE_NAME);

    return DjiUserUtil_RunSystemCmd(cmdStr);
}
```

### TTS Speaking

The complete flow for TTS speaking is: User enters or imports text on the Pilot side -> SDK transmits text data through the `ReceiveTtsData` callback -> Payload side performs TTS audio synthesis -> Play audio.

**Receive TTS Text Data**

The SDK transmits the text data to the payload device through the `ReceiveTtsData` callback. The transmission process is the same as voice speaking, also divided into START -> TRANSMIT -> FINISH stages, except that the transmitted content is text data rather than Opus audio.

**TTS Audio Synthesis**

After receiving the text data, the text needs to be converted to audio on the payload device side. The implementation needs to be adapted to the specific speaker payload hardware. There are two commonly used methods:

- Dedicated TTS hardware chip: This type of chip generally provides I2C or UART interface protocols. The text needs to be converted and sent according to the protocol format to complete audio playback.
- Software synthesis library: Synthesize the input text and convert it into audio data through a software library. This type of library has higher requirements on system resources and has a more mature solution, such as [Xunfei Offline Speech Synthesis SDK](https://www.xfyun.cn/doc/tts/offline_tts/Linux-SDK.html).

An open source TTS library - [Ekho](http://www.eguidedog.net/cn/ekho_cn.php) is used in the speaker Sample as a demonstration only. For better TTS results, other methods are recommended.

```c
    memset(cmdStr, 0, sizeof(cmdStr));

    /*! Attention: you can use other tts opensource function to convert txt to speech, example used ekho v7.5 */
    snprintf(cmdStr, sizeof(cmdStr), " ekho %s -s 20 -p 20 -a 100", data);

    return DjiUserUtil_RunSystemCmd(cmdStr);
```
